## 2. The store timeline is the thing that will surprise you

Two facts that reorder the whole plan:

**Google Play's closed-testing requirement.** Personal developer accounts must
run a closed test with a minimum number of real testers (12, historically)
opted in for 14 continuous days before you can even *apply* for production.
Verify the current rule at account creation, but assume it's real. That means
**Android launch needs 12 humans two weeks before launch**. This isn't a tax —
it's the first cohort. Recruit them as beta users, let them write the first
letters, and cold start + the Play requirement are solved by one action.

**Apple costs $99/year, Google $25 once.** The $99 is the real decision.
Windows dev machine means iOS builds *must* go through EAS cloud — no local
escape hatch. Recommendation: **ship Android first**, alone, for a month. Real
usage, real screenshots, real metrics for the write-up, and the $99 only gets
spent once there's something to show. Lithuania skews Android anyway.

**Review risk:** an anonymous social app with UGC and stranger-to-stranger chat
is exactly the category Apple scrutinises hardest (guideline 1.2). It needs
content filtering, in-app reporting, blocking, a published EULA, and a
commitment to act on reports within 24h. This app has *all of these already* —
keyword gate, single review queue, bidirectional blocking, hosted terms, 18+
gate. State that explicitly in the review notes. The safety model is the best
asset here; most solo apps get rejected precisely because they lack it.

## 3. Marketing: the product is deliberately anti-viral, and that's fine

Name this honestly in the write-up rather than pretending otherwise. Streaks,
badges, digests, and an inbox were all explicitly rejected. That is a
defensible design thesis *and* a growth handicap. Don't fight it — route
around it:

- **Earned media is the channel.** Lithuania is 2.8M people, and national media
  is reachable by one person with one email. A young solo dev shipping a
  Lithuanian-language app about anonymous letters and thank-yous pinned to real
  places is a *story* — 15min, Delfi, LRT, plus the lifestyle/psychology angle.
  Free, high-trust, hits the exact audience. One placement beats any affordable
  ad budget.
- **Map letters are the press-photo feature.** "Letters left at the spot in
  Vilnius where someone wanted to say thank you" is visual and screenshot-able.
  The random pool isn't.
- **Launch one city, not one country.** Density beats reach for both mechanics.
  A map with 40 letters in Vilnius looks alive; 40 spread across Lithuania
  looks abandoned. Same for the pool — `recipient_cap = ceil(users/16)`
  punishes a thin user base.
- **Facebook groups and TikTok, not paid ads.** Zero CAC is the only CAC that
  works for an app with no revenue. Reading real (consented) letters aloud over
  the envelope ceremony is native TikTok content and costs nothing but time.

## 4. The write-up is the actual deliverable for the real goal

The point is freelance work. Then **installs are not the metric — the case
study is.** 300 real users with an honest, well-instrumented narrative wins
more client work than 10,000 installs with no story. Two campaigns, two
audiences: the app markets to Lithuanians in Lithuanian; the case study markets
to hiring people in English, on LinkedIn / r/reactnative / the Expo community.

The one thing to start immediately: **`decisions.md`**. Cheapest thing that
decays fastest. Pull-based distribution over push, no GPS ever, toggleable
likes, quiet notifications, why DAU was rejected — the *why* is still fresh
now. In three months it won't be, and reconstructed reasoning reads as
reconstructed.

Also record the launch itself as it happens: tester recruitment, store review
back-and-forth, week-by-week numbers. A case study with a live timeline is far
more convincing than a retrospective.
